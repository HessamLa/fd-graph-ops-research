### Finding: `fdlinear` buys the GEOMETRY, and not the link prediction

The AUC of every `both` row sits in 0.9967..0.9989, a band of 0.22%. That
is noise at the floor of 2% that held then, and it stays noise at the
floor of 1% of 2026-08-17T23:19. **The force law does not decide the link
prediction on Cora.**

The hop R2 does separate, and the test is the delta against the SEED
SPREAD, not the percentage alone:

| policy | fdlinear | v1 | delta | vs the larger spread | verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `walk_edges/both` | 0.677 | 0.445 | **+52.2%** | 0.232 against 0.037, a factor of 6 | **real** |
| `nbr_walk/both` | 0.668 | 0.572 | **+16.7%** | 0.096 against 0.091 | **real, and MARGINAL** |
| `nbr_walk/low_deg` | 0.345 | 0.395 | -12.8% | 0.051 against 0.154 | **noise** |

The `walk_edges` result is decisive. The `nbr_walk/both` result passes both
halves of the rule and it passes the second half by a hair, thus it must
be quoted with its spread and never as "+17%" alone.

### Finding: `low_deg` costs the geometry, and it also DESTABILISES it

On Cora `low_deg` costs 48% of the hop R2 for `fdlinear` (0.668 -> 0.345).
The seed spread also grows from ±.012 to ±.064 for `fdlinear`, and from
±.046 to ±.077 for `v1`. **The rule makes the result depend on the seed.**
That is a second reason to reject it, beside the loss of the mean, and it
was not stated when the rule was proposed.

Mechanism, already recorded on 2026-08-16 02:30: the attraction lives at
`h = 1` ONLY. `low_deg` removes entries at `h = 1`, thus it removes
attraction, and the walk list that stays behind carries repulsion alone.

**Verdict: `low_deg` is rejected.** It buys 6.1% of the memory and it
sells 64% of the geometry and 6.2% of the accuracy. The adjacency coverage
falls to 54.6%, and the attraction lives at `h = 1` only.

**The idea behind the rule is still sound**, and this is worth keeping: an
asymmetric `D` DOES work. The 1.13M runs above are all asymmetric already
(axis E-C, directed semantics), and `nbr_walk/both` reaches AUC 0.9978
with it. What fails is not the asymmetry; it is dropping an ATTRACTION
term. A rule that drops far entries instead of `h = 1` entries would not
have this cost, and it is not tested.
