## 2026-08-24 -- `fdwalk/modular.py`, and what the reproduction exposed

**Ask.** "Implement `fdwalk/modular.py` in a modular style, similar to
`fodined/modular.py`. Copy the scripts from other directories instead of
writing from scratch. For the evaluation part, import the `evaluator`
package. Verify by embedding Cora and PubMed and obtaining the exact same
results found in `experiments/fdwalk/`."

**Thought, and the one decision that shaped the work.** `fodiwalk/` is the
packaged form of this experiment and it is parity-tested, thus it was the
obvious base. It cannot serve: `fodiwalk` REMOVED the `v1` force law on
2026-08-19 (`fodiwalk/dev-docs/CATALOG.md` section 13) and the `ball` and
`sampled` policies on 2026-08-20. The G1 and G2 tables are `v1` runs.
Reproducing them therefore needs the `fodined` engine, which still holds
`shell_force`, plus the walk augmentation of THIS directory.

**Action.**

* `fdwalk/walks.py` and `fdwalk/weights.py` -- verbatim copies, thus
  `diff` is the parity argument and no edit of mine can move a number.
* `fdwalk/modular.py` -- the configuration, the ORDER of the steps and the
  messages. The engine, the force law, the plan builder and the kernel are
  imported from `fodined`; the model class is copied from
  `fodined/modular.py`. `evaluator` gives every score.
* ONE generator, in the order of `bench_fdwalk.py`: the augmentation, then
  the link prediction, then the hop sample. `rng=` is passed to every
  `evaluator` call. The generator STATE is saved and restored between the
  two hop protocols, thus both feature widths read ONE sample, which is
  what `bench_fdwalk.py` does with one `hop_sample` and two `task_hop`.

**Result: Cora reproduces EXACTLY, three seeds, every field.** PubMed did
not, and the two findings that followed are in `FINDINGS.md`:

1. **This directory no longer reproduces its own G2 table.**
   `bench_fdwalk.py`, unmodified, run today, gives `D.nnz = 654,190`
   against the recorded 653,866. The cause is the `block` of
   `walk_pair_stats`: `uniform_walks` draws one `rng.random(block)` for
   each STEP, thus the partition of the start nodes decides which numbers
   of the stream reach which walk. The tables were made at 100,000 and
   `walks.py` holds 50,000 today. Cora is one block at both values, which
   is exactly why G1 reproduced and G2 did not.
2. **`r2_vec` carries about 0.012 of run-to-run noise**, measured on four
   identical runs with a bit-identical `Z`. The 128-feature MLP and the
   200-tree forest accumulate in thread order. `r2_dist`, `auc`, `dz` and
   `D.nnz` were identical in all four.

**A defect of `evaluator`, found and repaired.** Its `fodined` and
`fodiwalk_*` protocols drew negatives with the rejection sampler of
`bench_other_ge.py`, and both files they are named for call
`sample_far_pairs`. The two consume the generator differently, thus the
LINK PREDICTION pairs AND the hop sample that follows both change. Until
the repair the reproduction was impossible: `acc` 0.9777 against 0.9744,
hop R2 0.630 against 0.616. A third compatibility knob, `LPCfg.neg_draw`,
beside the `pos_draw` and `pair_draw` that file already carries.
`otherge` is unchanged and `fodined` is now bit-exact against
`fodined/link_prediction.py`.

**What is NOT reproduced, and it is stated rather than worked around:**
`h2_exact` and `h2_pairs`. `evaluator` holds no H2 task. Rebuilding one
inside the script would duplicate `hop_sample` and defeat the reason to
call the package. H2 measures the AUGMENTATION -- how tight the walk-gap
bound is -- and not the embedding.

## 2026-08-24 -- the investigation, and a correction of my own claim

**Ask.** Pass the four differing fields to the investigator session
`fdmap-b6`, with the file paths, and have it iterate until it finds the
reason. Do not investigate myself.

**Action.** Sent the numbers, the mine-against-recorded table, the four
repeat runs, both sides of every file to compare, the two facts already
established (`PAIR_BLOCK`, the `neg_draw` repair), five hypotheses, and
the one control run I had not done -- the original harness today at
`block = 100_000`.

**Result: my mechanism was WRONG.** I wrote that the spread came from
multi-threaded sklearn estimators. It does not. The scoring stack is
bit-deterministic on a fixed `Z`; `Z` itself is not reproducible, because
`dZ.at[rows].add(...)` is a scatter and a split hub row sends several
adds to ONE address.

**Verified here, not taken on report.** Two tests, re-run in this session:

1. `at[idx].add` with `k` duplicate indices, 8 calls: `k=1` and `k=2` give
   ONE result; `k=3` gives 6 and `k=4` gives 8. The threshold is exactly 3,
   because two float32 addends commute and three do not associate.
2. The max owner-id multiplicity inside one batch: Cora `{1: 15, 2: 1}`,
   PubMed `{1: 54, 4: 1}`. Cora never reaches 3. PubMed does.

That is the size asymmetry I asked about, and it is not about size: it is
whether one hub row splits into 3+ virtual rows inside ONE batch.

**What I take from it.** The claim "a quality metric is deterministic
given the seed" (`CLAUDE.md`) holds for `r2_dist`, `auc`, `dz` and
`D.nnz`, and NOT for `acc`, `f1`, `r2_vec`, `mae_vec` on a graph whose
plan splits a hub three ways. `fdwalk/modular.py` has no defect; the
record it is measured against is unreachable by the original harness too.

**Not applied, and recorded as open:** raising `k_max` to remove the split,
and the docstring of `sell_c_sigma.py:11-22`, which states the layout has
"no scatter, no atomics". Both belong to the engine and to a decision of
the user.
