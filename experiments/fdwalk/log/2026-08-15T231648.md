### The results, in short

* **G1, Cora, 30 runs.** `walk/min_gap` reaches an AUC of 0.9970 ±.0009 and
  a hop R2 of 0.655 ±.028. node2vec on the same protocol reaches 0.324.
* **G2, PubMed, 9 runs.** AUC 0.9953 ±.0002, R2 0.549 ±.032. node2vec
  reaches 0.080, thus fdwalk is 6.9 times better on that measure.
* **G3, com_youtube, 1.13M nodes.** FAILED. Three attempts, all inside the
  augmentation, at 20 s, 32 s, and 152 s.

### The findings that direct the work now

1. The weight 1 must be the adjacency of the graph. `min_gap` gives it
   (10,550 of 10,556 directed edges of Cora). `mean_gap` gives 142 entries
   at the weight 1, and the embedding then collapses to an AUC of 0.55.
2. The pair set decides the link prediction, and the WEIGHT decides the
   geometry. H3 predicted the opposite.
3. The walk gap is an upper bound of the hop distance, and it is exact for
   98.1% of the stored pairs of Cora. It is never under, which is a proof
   and not a measurement.
4. A budget on the OUTPUT is not a budget on the PIPELINE. The cap bounds
   `D`. It does not bound the accumulator of distinct pairs, and that is
   what stopped G3.

### The decisions

* The ball stops at every gate (decision of the user, 2026-08-15). It needs
  50 GB at k=2 on com_youtube, thus no budget reaches it.
* `mean_gap` and `pmi` stop after G1.
* `walk_edges` and `walk` are one variant, and `walk` continues.
* I stopped the G3 attempts at three, by the rule that I fixed before the
  third one started. The one constant that stands between attempt 3 and a
  pass (`prune_factor`) was NOT changed, because changing it would have
  been tuning until a pass.

### The plan from here

## 2026-08-15 23:20 PDT -- landmarks, the first result: WORSE

**Thought:** every far pair takes the same weight, 100. A pair at 6 hops
and a pair at 19 hops are then the same number to the force law. 50 exact
BFS give a real distance through the triangle inequality
`d(u,v) <= min_L d(u,L) + d(L,v)`, and it costs 18 s at 1.13M nodes.

**Action:** wrote `landmarks.py` (pick, distances, pair_distance) and added
`--landmarks` and `--far-max` to the harness. Ran Cora, seed 42.

**Result:** the weights now run 1..17 and not 1..5 plus 100. And the result
fell:

| Metric | constant 100 | landmarks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AUC | 0.9958 | 0.9964 |
| R2 dist | **0.616** | **0.195** |
| R2 vec | 0.789 | 0.751 |

A better distance gave a worse embedding. That is a result to explain, and
not a result to hide.

## 2026-08-15 23:30 PDT -- H7, and the mechanism

**Thought:** two candidate causes. (1) The SCALE: repulsion is linear in
`h`, thus 6..17 pushes 10 times weaker than 100. (2) The ATTRACTION band:
`Fa` falls with `exp(-k2 * (h - h_shift))`, thus `h = 100` gives exactly
zero attraction, and `h = 4` gives `exp(-3) = 0.05`, which is not zero.

H7 of PLAN.md says that the far weight is not sensitive. It was never
tested. Test it now: the constant at 10, 100, and 1000.

**Action:** two runs, Cora, seed 42, `--far-weight 10` and `1000`.

**Result:**

| far weight | AUC | R2 dist | final dZ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | 0.9959 | 0.641 | 4.2095 |
| 100 | 0.9958 | 0.616 | 0.2711 |
| 1000 | 0.9952 | 0.239 | 0.2711 |
| landmarks, 4..17 | 0.9964 | 0.195 | 0.2709 |

**H7 is refuted.** The far weight is very sensitive: 0.641 to 0.239 over
two orders of magnitude.

And the SCALE alone does not explain the landmarks: a constant of 10 gives
0.641, and the landmark weights near 4..17 give 0.195. Thus cause (2) is
the one. The far pairs are RANDOM pairs, and 18,590 of them. At a weight
near 4 they attract each other, and that attraction is noise: it pulls
together pairs that are only "far".

**The rule that comes out of it:** the weight of a far pair must sit
OUTSIDE the attraction band. `exp(-k2 * (h - 1))` with `k2 = 1` is 0.05 at
h = 4, and 1e-13 at h = 30. Thus the order of the far pairs is useful only
if the whole range sits above about 20.

## 2026-08-16 00:05 PDT -- D1, the chunked plan, works and it is exact

**Thought:** the kernel writes `dZ.at[rows].add(...)`. A cut by ROWS gives
each chunk a disjoint set of rows, thus no sum crosses a chunk. And
`Fodined.embed` already cuts `dZ` by a row range for its batches, thus the
chunk and the batch are the same object, and the engine of the package
needs NO change. A cut by pairs inside a row would need a sum across the
chunks, and it would need a change of the kernel.

Three quantities stay global, and they must: `shell_coeff_data`,
`degrees_from_D`, and `inv_deg_ext`. A degree that counts one chunk is not
the degree of the node. The chunk only SLICES those arrays.

**Action:** `augment_graph` now builds one plan for each row range, and
`forces` picks the plan of the row range that the batch asks for.
`--chunks` and `--chunk-host`.

**Result:** Cora, 300 epochs, seed 42:

| chunks | plan cells | AUC | R2 dist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 75,334 | 0.9970 | 0.620 |
| 4 | 75,334 | 0.9970 | 0.627 |

The cell count is IDENTICAL, which is the exact check: the chunks hold
every stored pair and no pair two times. The R2 moves by 0.007 because the
random drop takes one key for each batch, thus 4 chunks draw 4 keys where 1
chunk draws 1. The physics is the same, and the random stream is not.
