## 2026-08-16 01:10 PDT -- the off-branch experiment

**Ask:** a new force law, and a new augmentation budget.

```
h == 1:  F = Fa + Fr,  Fr = -k3*h*exp(-k4*x)
h  > 1:  F = Fr,       Fr = -k3*h          (no decay with the distance)

Augmentation: every original edge, plus n*log10(n) pairs at
              50% h=2, 25% h=3, 25% h>=4.
Thus |D| = |E| + |V|*log10(|V|).
```

Try the ball on Cora and PubMed, and the walk on Cora, PubMed, and the
1.13M graph.

**Thought, and one decision that the ask forces:** the size formula
`|E| + |V|*log10(|V|)` leaves NO room for the random far pairs at the
weight 100. Thus this branch has no far pairs and no sentinel, and every
`h` in `D` is a real hop distance or a real walk gap. The bucket `h >= 4`
must then come from real pairs, thus:

* the ball needs `k = 4`, because a ball at `k = 3` holds no pair at 4;
* the walk needs `window = 5`, for the same reason.

**Thought about the physics of the change:** in the law of the package the
repulsion of every pair falls with `exp(-k4 * x)`, thus a pair that is
already far stops pushing. The new law removes that decay for `h > 1`, thus
those pairs become a CONSTANT outward pressure. `Fa` is linear in `x`, thus
an edge now has a true equilibrium: the spring grows with the distance and
the push does not.

**Action:** `force_offbranch.py` (the law), `buckets.py` (the policy), and
two flags of the harness, `--force v2` and `--policy buckets`. The old
behaviour is the default, thus no earlier result moves.

**Result of the first check, Cora with the ball at k = 4, 300 epochs:**


## 2026-08-16 02:30 PDT -- the h levels of Fa and Fr, verified

**Ask:** "When we only consider immediate neighbors for `Fa`, then we only

**Action:** called `shell_force` directly over a range of `h`, instead of

**Result:**

| h | shell_coeff | x | Fa | Fr | F total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 (pad) | 0.00 | 2.0 | 0.000000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| 1 | 0.25 | 2.0 | 0.499500 | -9.8020 | -9.3025 |
| 2 | 0.25 | 2.0 | 0.000000 | -19.6040 | -19.6040 |
| 3 | 0.25 | 2.0 | 0.000000 | -29.4060 | -29.4060 |
| 5 | 0.25 | 2.0 | 0.000000 | -49.0099 | -49.0099 |
| 100 | 0.25 | 2.0 | 0.000000 | -980.1987 | -980.1987 |

* **Fa: h = 1 ONLY.** The guard is `where(h > 1, 0, 1)`.
* **Fr: EVERY h that D stores**, and it is linear in `h`.
* `shell_coeff` is computed for every entry at its own `h`, thus the
  coefficients of the entries at `h >= 2` are computed and then multiplied
  by zero. It is waste, and it is not an error.

**k2 is a DEAD parameter.** `Fa` carries `exp(-k2*(h - h_shift))` with
`h_shift = 1`, and `Fa` is non-zero only at `h = 1`, thus the exponent is
always `exp(0) = 1`. Measured: `k2 = 0.01`, `1.0`, and `100.0` all give
`F(h=1, x=2) = -9.302486`, the same to six decimals. Any sweep of `k2` in
this configuration measures nothing.

**A second consequence, and it is not small.** At `h = 1` the net force is
REPULSIVE at a short distance: at `x = 2` and degree 4 it is `+0.4995`
attraction against `-9.8020` repulsion. `Fa` grows with `x` and `Fr` decays
with `x`, thus an edge has an equilibrium far away:

| degree of the node | equilibrium `x*` of one h=1 pair |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | 9.1 |
| 4 | 29.7 |
| 40 | 120.3 |
| 400 | 269.8 |
| 28,754 | 614.9 |

The rest length of an edge therefore GROWS with the degree, because the
attraction carries `1/deg` and the repulsion does not. A hub sits about 20
times further from its neighbours than a leaf does. The engine then divides
the whole row sum by `deg1(u)` a second time, thus every force on a hub,
attraction AND repulsion, is small.

That is a third mechanism for the open question of the 1.13M AUC, beside
the uniform negative sampling: the geometry of a hub is set by a force that
is divided by its degree two times.

**Action:** the three readings of "asymmetric D" are now in PLAN.md as
axis E, with the note that the third law is ALREADY broken by the per-row
normalisation, and with the user's note about a cycle that does not
converge.

start node, and then `cap_per_node` keeps only the `m` partners with the
highest count. Two defects against the specification:

* A neighbour can be missing. Measured at 1.13M nodes: 4,036,102 entries at
  `h = 1` against 5,975,248 directed edges, thus **68%**, and a third of
  the adjacency was absent.
* The pairs are not "the nodes of ITS walk". They are the pairs of a window
  of every walk, thus row `u` could hold two nodes that a walk reached
  while `u` was not in it.

**The replacement, `--pairs nbr_walk`.** Row `u` holds:

1. EVERY neighbour of `u`, at `h = 1`, taken from `A` and not from a walk.
2. Every node that a walk STARTING AT `u` reached, at `h` = the first step
   at which it arrived.

No cap, no window. The budget is `n_walks * walk_len`, thus
`D.nnz <= n * n_walks * walk_len` by construction. `D` is DIRECTED: row `u`
holds what `u` found, which is axis E-B of PLAN.md, and the user approved
the asymmetric update.

A second gain, and it is not small: the pairs of a block of start nodes
belong only to those rows, thus NO block must merge with another. The
accumulator that stopped G3 three times cannot exist here, and no prune
approximates anything.

**Verified on Cora:** 10,556 entries at `h = 1` against 10,556 directed
edges of `A`, thus **100.0%**. `h` runs 1..19, and a row holds 44.6 entries.

**`fdlinear`.** Two readings of the specification were open, and both are
recorded in `force_fdlinear.py`:

* `exp(z_v - z_u)` reads as `exp(+x)`, which GROWS with the distance and
  cannot settle. The default is `exp(-k4 * x)`, and `--fdlinear-sign +1`
  gives the literal form.
* `freq` is the count of the PAIR by default (how often the walks of row
  `u` reached `v`), and `--freq-mode node` gives the count of the node.

`Fa = k * (z_v - z_u)` carries NO `1/deg`, thus `--no-deg-norm` passes
`degrees = 1` to `make_plan` and the engine does not divide the row sum
either.

**The sweep, Cora, 300 epochs, `nbr_walk`:**

| lr | deg norm | k4 | \|\|dZ\|\| | AUC | R2 dist |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.0 | on | 1.0 | 0.1715 | 0.9983 | **0.436** |
| 0.1 | on | 1.0 | 0.0491 | 0.9988 | 0.256 |
| 0.01 | off | 1.0 | 0.5135 | **0.9990** | 0.217 |

| 1.0, 0.1 | off | any | NaN | -- | -- |
| 0.001 | on | 1.0 | 5.4124 | 0.6725 | -0.005 |

Two results of the sweep:

1. **`k4 = 1.0` beats `k4 = 0.01` on every row.** `exp(-x)` is the literal
   reading of the specification, and it is also the better one.
2. **The engine must keep its `1/deg`.** Without it the law diverges to NaN
   at `lr >= 0.1`, because `Fa = k * x` over a row is a raw sum and the
   step is then proportional to the degree.

## 2026-08-17 04:20 PDT -- the low-degree edge rule, and two defects of my own

**Ask:** an edge `(u, v)` enters the list of `u` only when
`deg(v) >= deg(u)`, and the walk list of `u` enters too. Mix and match
every method. Commit and push.

**The rule, and what it saves.** `--edge-rule low_deg`. A leaf keeps its
edge to a hub, and the hub does not keep the same edge. On Cora: 7,920
entries at `h = 1` against 10,556, thus **25% fewer**. It is not 50%,
because an edge between two nodes of EQUAL degree enters both rows, and a
sparse graph has many such ties.

**A trap in this rule, and the engine hides it.** A hub can now hold NO
entry at `h = 1`. `degrees_from_D` counts exactly those entries, thus it
returns 0 for that row, and `inv_deg_ext` turns a 0 into 0.0. The kernel
multiplies the WHOLE row force by that number, thus every force of the hub
would be zero, the repulsion too, and the node would never move. Nothing
would raise an error; the hub would simply stay where it started.

`--deg-source auto` therefore gives `make_plan` the degree of `A`, and not
the count of `D`. The `degrees` parameter of `make_plan` exists for exactly
this, and its docstring says so: "what counts as a degree is a force-law
question".

**Defect 1, mine, found by the histogram.** With a DIRECTED `D`, the far
pairs collided with the walk pairs. `sample_far_pairs` rejects on the
directed key, thus a pair stored as `(v, u)` did not reject `(u, v)`, and
the CSR build SUMMED the two: the histogram showed weights of 101..119,
which is `100 + the walk gap`. About 0.18% of the entries, and it also
touched the `nbr_walk + far` result of 03:10. The far pairs are now
filtered against BOTH directions.

**Defect 2, mine, found by the grid.** I attached the `freq` plane to every
run, and `shell_force` of the package unpacks exactly two planes, thus
every `v1` run stopped with "too many values to unpack". Three runs of the
grid were lost. The plane now goes only to `fdlinear`, and a smoke test of
all six combinations at 20 epochs runs before the grid does.

epochs, far pairs at `deg^0.75` for every variant.
